# An Entity's identity is a canonical key, not the name an outlet wrote

Extraction used to read a run of title-case words and key the result on the string it found, so an Entity existed only in exactly the spelling the first Article happened to use. That is a decision about what an Entity *is*, not only about how one is found, and it is what made the Entity vocabulary described in the glossary — the key used for Cluster overlap and the unit of Trends — true for one kind of name and not for another.

An all-caps name ("IBM") and a mixed-case one ("eBay", "McDonald") were not extracted at all, so half of what outlets write was invisible. A multi-word name was emitted as one run only when every word was title-case, so a possessive truncated it ("Acme Corp's shares" gave "Acme"). And the same company written "Acme Corp" in one Article and "ACME CORPORATION" in another became two Entities, which share nothing, so the overlap that both clustering and trends are measured on was zero for exactly the names that recur across outlets — silently, because nothing in the pipeline reported a miss.

The Entity is now identified by a **canonical key**: the name with its capitalisation, punctuation, leading article and legal form folded away. `canonicalEntityKey` is the whole of the folding, it is a pure function of the name, and it is stored beside the name in its own unique column. A name keeps the spelling an outlet used — the first one, because that is the form an Article leads with and the form a User reads — and the key is what two Articles are matched on. So "Acme Corp", "ACME CORP.", "Acme Corporation" and "Acme" are one Entity, and "Acme Foods" is another.

The unique index on the name stays, and it is no longer what decides identity: two rows can never share a name, because a name arrives with a key and the second one resolves to the first row. The index on the key is the one that can be violated by a legacy database, which is why the migration derives keys *before* the DDL rather than with the backfills that run after it — and merges the pairs an older build wrote as two rows, since an Entity that exists twice cannot be keyed once.

Three further decisions inside the same change, each of which the glossary's sentence about Entities depends on:

- **Candidates are generated generously and filtered afterwards.** Runs never cross a comma or a sentence boundary, a connector is carried inside the run rather than stepped over ("Bank of England" is one name and the scan resumes after it), and a legal suffix ends a name rather than beginning the next, because "Jane Doe, Acme Corp" is two names and "Acme Corp Foo" is a company and a product. Without those, maximal munch glues whatever capitals happen to be adjacent, and stepping over a connector leaves the scan pointing into the middle of the name it just read.
- **A name at the head of a sentence is kept when the Article says it twice, or when the word names itself.** A capitalised word at the start of a sentence is capitalised because that is where a capital goes, and "Rainfall", "Then" and "New" are common words in news prose. Repetition is the other signal that separates a name from a capital letter, and unlike a dictionary of English it needs no lexicon. This is what drops "New Acme" — the same Acme, said two ways in one Article — while keeping "Foo" in a headline the body repeats.
- **A cue that ends in a connective does not name the thing that follows it.** "The acquisition of TinyCo" is TinyCo, and "the central bank of Belvern" is a bank in Belvern: read the second the same way as the first and every country with a central bank of that name becomes one.
- **An Entity's kind is classified, and the first Article that named it decides.** A kind read off a rule cascade is a guess, so a name no rule places is a concept: that kind says only that this is a named thing, which is true even when it is the only thing known about it. A later Article reading it differently does not overwrite it, because the kind is a fact about the Entity rather than about the mention. Nothing downstream depends on the kind yet — grouping is decided on which Entities two Stories share — and every Entity stored as `concept` was the vocabulary going unused while the column claimed to be classified.

The rules are shallow on purpose: no model, and no dictionary of every English word. What replaces the dictionary is a measured pair of guards over the wire-copy fixtures in `src/testing/story-fixtures.ts` — every rewrite of one story shares the Entities that identify it, and no two different stories share enough of theirs to be grouped — which fail if the lexicons stop covering the text, rather than passing because the fixtures happen to be the ones the rules were written against. The lexicons live in their own module (`entity-lexicons.ts`) precisely because they are the part most likely to need editing, and the two things that would show an edit was wrong are in a different file.

Three things this leaves as they were, all deliberate. An Entity is still stored with the name an outlet wrote, so the name a User reads is a real outlet's spelling rather than a normalised string, and the only aliasing is the folding the key does — "the Fed" and "the Federal Reserve" are still two Entities, and nothing here would notice that they should be one. Extraction is a rule cascade over a lexicon, so a name mentioned once, at the head of a sentence, with no capital of its own, is not extracted ("Nvidia beats forecasts on record revenue" yields nothing); that is the cost of not needing a dictionary, and the price is a missed Entity rather than a wrong one, because a wrong name is a story that does not group. And the kind comes from a cascade that ends in `concept`, so a name it cannot place is filed as one — "Narendra Modi" is a concept here unless `Modi` reaches the given-name list, and the vocabulary is reached because the rules cover it rather than because a gazetteer was loaded.
