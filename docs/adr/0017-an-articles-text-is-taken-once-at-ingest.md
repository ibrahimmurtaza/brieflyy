# An Article's text is what the Source wrote, taken once at ingest

[[0007-story-identity-is-a-stored-signature]] decides what a Story signature is and how two of them are compared. It does not say what text the signature is taken from, and the answer it left implicit turned out to be the whole defect.

An Article's body is whatever the feed put in `<description>`, and for a good number of the registry's Sources that is not prose. hnrss.org serves the feed's own bookkeeping instead of a description — `Article URL: … Comments URL: … Points: 33 # Comments: 10`. nature.com serves a real standfirst behind a fixed citation header naming the publication, the date and the DOI. Both were already known, and both were already handled: `cluster-text.ts` cut them out of the sentences it quoted back to a User, with the measurement recorded — 1,098 sentences from a day's ingest across all twenty Sources, in which the two cuts removed every Hacker News metadata blob and every Nature citation line and no real prose.

That was the whole of the handling, and it was in the renderer. Nothing cut that text before it was signed or read for Entities, and those are the two things that decide what the pipeline believes:

- The word pattern discards digits, so `Points: 6 # Comments: 0` differs from `Points: 328 # Comments: 163` only in characters no signature carries. Seventy-six unrelated Hacker News Articles scored as one Story, and a Cluster of them was titled *I stopped reviewing my agents' code* with a bullet about an obituary.
- `Points` and `Comments` are ordinary capitalised words to a rule that reads runs of them, so they joined into one name. Of the six most-mentioned Entities in a day of live ingest, four were `Points Comments`, `Comments`, `URL` and `Article`. An Entity is what two Stories are matched on, so a name that appears in every Article of a feed matches every Article of that feed — which is the mechanism behind the 76-Article Story, not a cosmetic problem alongside it.
- The Nature header is most of the text it prefixes, so twenty-two unrelated Nature Articles were one Story, and `Nature Published`, `Published` and `September` were Entities too.

A defect that lives in the renderer is a defect waiting to be invisible: the surfaces a User looks at were correct, and every test signed hand-written prose.

The decision is therefore that an Article's text is established once, where it is taken in, and everything downstream reads that. `feed-text.ts` owns it, `rss-parser` applies it, and the two other readers of an Article's text go through it rather than each cutting their own — `cluster-text.ts`, which still needs it for rows written before this, and `extractEntities`, which needs it because a rule that reads bookkeeping as a name is wrong wherever the text arrives from.

Three consequences follow, and two of them are the interesting part.

**The signature covers the headline as well as the body.** They were joined differently: Entities were read from `title\nbody` while the signature was read from the body alone. That was not a decision anybody had made — it is what the code did. A feed with no description gives a headline and nothing else, so signing the body alone signs an empty signature, which by [[0007-story-identity-is-a-stored-signature]]'s own rule matches nothing, so every such Article becomes its own Story while the one thing the outlet actually said goes unread. `articleText` is now the single definition, used by ingest and by the boot-time backfill alike — a backfill deriving a signature by a different rule writes a key the running build does not produce, and the next Article placed against that Story is compared on different terms to a freshly-ingested one.

**Key phrases are per sentence.** A two- and three-word window over the Article's whole word list straddles the end of one sentence and the start of the next, and the last words of one sentence joined to the first words of the next are a phrase nobody wrote. It counts for exactly as much against a threshold as a phrase that was, and a syndication pass breaks sentences in a different place from the copy it is rewriting, so a straddling phrase is among the first things a genuine rewrite loses. The words stay whole-Article, because a rewrite replaces a sentence here and there and the rest still counts. This moved both margins the right way — the same-story floor rose from 0.205 to 0.207 and the different-story ceiling from 0.089 to 0.091 — which is the shape of a change that removes noise rather than one that trades precision for recall.

**A stored signature is not re-derived, so a database written before this is wrong until it is rebuilt.** The backfill in `migrate.ts` reaches only rows the column migration left as `'{}'`, and says so: a signature a newer build wrote is left exactly as it is. Combined with ingest skipping an Article it has already seen by external id, no amount of re-polling repairs it, and a signature is a cache of what the Article's text says — so the cache is stale rather than the text. Clusters recover on their own, because cluster formation re-derives them from the Stories on every cycle; Stories do not. The fix for an affected database is to rebuild it, and a build that has to work against one in place needs a signature version, which this does not add: nothing in the product is currently repaired in place, and a version column would be a mechanism for a migration nobody has asked for.
