# A Story spans Sources

ADR-0007 left one thing as it was, deliberately: "A Story belongs to one Source, so a story syndicated across outlets is one Story per outlet rather than one Story overall." The stated reason was that ADR-0003 makes the Source the unit of ingestion, deduplication and failure, and that collapsing across Sources "would put one outlet's outage or rewrite rate on every other outlet's Stories". That reason is about ingestion and failure, and it still holds there. It was carried one step further than it goes, and the step it took was the one the whole Story grain exists to avoid.

A Story is the story, not one outlet's account of it. A syndication desk rewrites one report for every outlet that will carry it — the headline replaced, a clause turned around, a quote moved to an unnamed source, the tail padded — and a Story whose dedup was scoped to one Source admitted each of those rewrites as a new Story. So the most-covered story in the corpus was the one that arrived as the most separate Stories, which is the opposite of what the grain is for. The failure rate the argument was protecting against was in the pipeline rather than in the model: one outlet's outage is one fewer Article in a Story that already exists, and an outage was never going to be blamed on a Story it did not contribute to.

Nothing about identity needed the Source. ADR-0007 made a Story's identity its stored signature, fixed by the first Article that formed it and compared rather than rehashed, so a copy arriving from any outlet is compared on its own merits and the outcome does not depend on which feed was polled first. The Source was a filter on the *candidate set*, which is not the same thing as the thing being decided, and it was the only thing standing between the comparison and every Story published near the Article.

The constraint was in the schema rather than only in the query. `stories.source_id` was `NOT NULL REFERENCES sources(id)`, and a unique index on `(source_id, fingerprint)` depended on it; the dedup lookup filtered on it, and `listBySourceIdsInWindow` — the Topic-to-Stories lookup cluster formation is built on — filtered on it as well. The second one is worth separating out, because it was failing quietly in a direction nothing reported: a Story was in scope for a Topic only if the Source that *formed* it was one the Topic follows, so syndicated coverage was reachable from one Topic and invisible from every other.

Three decisions inside the change:

- **The column is retired, not made nullable.** Which Sources a Story belongs to is a fact about the Articles in it, and there is exactly one place that knows it. A column left behind would name one of several, could be filtered on again by the next reader, and would reintroduce this same bug by accident with nothing left to catch it — nullable would have kept the trap and dropped the constraint, which is the worst of both. `RETIRED_COLUMNS` drops the column and its index, and the foreign key goes with it: a Story is no longer something a Source owns, and cascading a Source's deletion into Stories that other outlets also reported would delete reporting that had nothing to do with it.
- **A Story's Sources are read off its Articles.** `hydrateMany` already counted them for the article count, so the grouped query it runs now collects the Sources in the same pass rather than a query per Story. `listBySourceIdsInWindow` joins through `articles` for the same reason, which is what makes a Story found by the Source that did not form it. A Story with no Articles yet has no Sources, which is the truth about it: the first Article is written a line after the Story is.
- **A Topic's Clusters are still built only from Sources it follows.** This is what the widened lookup would otherwise have broken. A syndicated Story is ingested once for the whole registry, and a Topic that follows only one of the outlets in it has no business being shown the other one's copy: grouping on Entities the User cannot see would fuse two Stories because of a mention from an outlet they never added, and a Cluster summary is only ever quoted from Articles the Cluster has. So formation and the read path both narrow a Story's Articles to the Topic's Sources — which is what a Story's Articles used to be for free, when a Story had exactly one — and a Cluster's Source list, its quoted text, its article links and the source filter a User is offered are all Sources they follow. Which outlets reported something is a fact about the reporting; who is shown it is a fact about the Topic.

Two more things this leaves as they were, both deliberate. The candidate set is every Story published inside the window rather than one feed's, which grows with how much the whole registry published in three days; the window is unchanged, its upper bound is the indexed one, and at the volume the pipeline works at that is a set of Stories compared pairwise against one Article — the comparison ADR-0007 was written to make affordable. And a Story's signature is still set once, by the Article that created it, and does not grow as copies arrive: a Story whose identity shifted every time a new outlet carried it would be a different Story each time, and the copies that were the reason it existed would not match it.

The declared schema and the applied DDL now have to agree on nullability as well as on columns, foreign keys and index uniqueness, because a `NOT NULL` that one of them has and the other does not is a constraint that stops being true of the database while the code that reads it still assumes it. The guard that compares them skips primary keys, where the two schemas say the same thing in different words and there is no disagreement to detect.
